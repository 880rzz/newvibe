About Alexandra

Take your time. Every path starts somewhere.

Diófa Műhely · Sajbán-Frőhner Alexandra

I love helping. I really do.

I love working with children, organising things and finding a way to help when someone needs it. I like knowing that something I have done has made a difference. And yet, some evenings I realise that everyone has had a piece of my day while the promises I made to myself have quietly slipped away.

There is room for me in the story, too

Sometimes I say yes before I have even stopped to ask whether I really have time. I am learning to say no, to notice my own limits and to ask for help when I need it. I do not have an answer to every problem. What I do have are stories, questions and everyday experiences that might sound familiar to someone else.

That is where Egy kis tér began: with 24 recognisable situations, small questions and pages you can return to without having to work through them in order. I want a person opening one of my publications to feel that a few honest words can be enough. With children, I care just as much about making space for a conversation and knowing when to turn to a trusted adult.

How I make these publications

I usually start with a real-life moment: an answer that will not come, a task that feels too big, a child who needs to be heard, or a quiet moment that never seems to arrive. I turn it into questions, stories, practical writing pages or an activity that someone can make their own. The thirty-day prayer journal, the planning books and the conversation cards have different purposes, but the same starting point: everyday life.

The initial ideas, my personal experiences and the editorial selection are mine. I have used AI assistance for some text editing, illustrations and digital cover previews. Where a story draws on something I have lived through, I distinguish it from an invented example; suggested conversations are possibilities to try, not transcripts of real exchanges. I take responsibility for the final editorial choices, without suggesting that the publications are clinical tools or carry official institutional or church endorsement.

The Helyed van köztünk cards are shown in real product photographs. Other cover visuals are clearly identified as digital illustrations. The original publications are in Hungarian; the English, German and French pages explain them but do not represent translated print editions.
